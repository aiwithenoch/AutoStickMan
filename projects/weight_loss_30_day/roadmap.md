# A practical 30-day weight-management roadmap

This is a general habit-building guide, not a personalized medical plan. Weight change varies by person. If you are pregnant, under 18, have a medical condition or injury, take medicine that affects weight, or have a history of disordered eating, speak with a qualified health professional before changing your eating or activity routine.

## Before day 1

Choose one reason you want to improve your health. Write down your usual meals, drinks, movement, sleep, and energy for a few days. This is information, not a judgment.

## Week 1 — Observe and simplify

- Replace sugary drinks with water or an unsweetened option most of the time.
- Build meals around vegetables or fruit, a protein food, and a satisfying whole-grain or other carbohydrate.
- Take an easy walk on most days, starting at a level you can repeat safely.
- Keep a simple record of meals, drinks, movement, sleep, and how you feel.

## Week 2 — Plan the easy choices

- Make a short shopping list before you shop.
- Keep convenient foods you enjoy available: fruit, vegetables, beans, eggs, yogurt, nuts, fish, poultry, or other protein foods that fit your needs.
- Prepare one or two repeatable meals so busy days do not decide everything for you.
- Gradually work toward the adult activity target of 150 minutes of moderate activity per week if it is appropriate for you.

## Week 3 — Add strength and support

- Add muscle-strengthening activity on two days if it is safe for you; use bodyweight, resistance bands, light weights, or another suitable option.
- Ask a friend or family member to walk, cook, or check in with you.
- Protect sleep and notice stress triggers that affect eating or movement.

## Week 4 — Review and continue

- Review what you actually repeated, not just what you planned.
- Keep the two or three habits that helped most.
- Change one barrier at a time instead of starting over after an imperfect day.
- Continue the routine beyond day 30; the goal is a pattern you can maintain.

## Safety note

Avoid starvation, detoxes, and unverified miracle pills. Stop and seek professional advice for concerning symptoms, pain, dizziness, fainting, or a pattern of restrictive or compensatory eating.
